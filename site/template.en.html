    About the charter

    We score whether crypto projects keep their promises, and whether anything makes them keep them, with one yardstick, in public. In stock markets, exchanges and bond trustees play the role of enforcer. Token Charter fills that role with public data now, and with collateral and automatic penalties later.

    Corrections and rating requests

    This ledger grows through everyone's corrections. Add one source URL; no name needed. What you send becomes a charter issue within a day, and people check the source and update the score if it holds up.

    Who enforces promises in stock markets

    Companies keep their promises because someone penalizes them when they don't. Crypto has no one in that role.

    • Stock market mechanismIPO lock-ups (usually 180 days in the US)Enforcer: Underwriter contracts→ Insider selling rules
    • Stock market mechanismPre-filed trading plans (US 10b5-1)Enforcer: Securities regulator→ Published selling plans
    • Stock market mechanismDividend policy, buyback resolutionsEnforcer: Board, shareholder meeting→ Holder-return commitments and delivery
    • Stock market mechanismTimely disclosure, quarterly reportsEnforcer: Exchange, regulator→ Regular reporting
    • Stock market mechanismBond covenantsEnforcer: Bond trustee, courts→ Collateral and automatic penalties
    • Stock market mechanismCorporate governance codesEnforcer: Exchange (comply or explain)→ Accountability
    • Stock market mechanismTokyo Stock Exchange list of companies disclosing capital-efficiency plansEnforcer: Public list (no penalty)→ Permissionless rating

    Roadmap

    1. Permissionless ratingScore from public data and publish. Not taking part becomes information in itself.
    2. Sources and corrections We are hereEvery score links its sources. Anyone can correct it with a source and 2 approvals. We are at this stage now.
    3. Registered promisesProjects register their return, selling and unlock commitments in a fixed format.
    4. Collateralized promisesProjects post collateral that goes to holders, by on-chain rules, if a promise is broken.

    Out of scope: hack prevention, market moves, and whether there is demand. Token Charter rates the reliability of promises only.

    Scores are based on public information and change only through sourced proposals with 2 approvals. Not investment advice.
